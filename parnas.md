1. Briefly describe one important lesson about modularity that the paper describes and that
you think is still relevant today.
- To me, the most important lesson gathered from the text is that basing your modules around the processing steps is irrelevant, and that, rather, when designing your modules, you should design them based on design decisions likely to change. The author calls this "information hiding"; each module conceals one decision behind an interface that reveals as little as possible. This is very relevant today in how coding and software development have evolved; this isolative modularization allows for easier debugging in a way that makes it so one change does not ripple across the entire program.
2. Computing has advanced significantly since this paper was written. Briefly describe one
challenge (not necessarily with modularity) or perspective that, while realistic in 1972,
does not apply today.
- A challenge not seen as much today is that of memory. He talks about how limited memory is, and the optimization of the little memory they had. Now, while this holds up now, it is much less of a concern; people mainly focus on the structure of the code, and then they focus on optimization since getting a computer with a good amount of memory is a lot more accessible now.
